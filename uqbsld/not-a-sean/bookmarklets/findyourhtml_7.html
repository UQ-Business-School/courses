Find your HTML

List every HTML block, H5P activity and tool link across your Blackboard courses, and find out exactly where each one lives.

What this is

A small tool that reads your Blackboard courses and writes down what it finds.

Blackboard's accessibility report will tell you that a course contains eleven HTML items. It will not tell you where any of them are. So when something needs checking or fixing, the only way to find them is to open every folder and look.

This does that part for you. It goes through the courses you have access to and produces a list of every HTML block, every H5P activity embedded in a document, and every tool link, with the folder path, the document it sits in, whether students can see it, and a link that opens it.

Before you worry about running a script

  • It only reads. There is nothing in it that writes, edits, hides or deletes, and it never opens the HTML editor, so it cannot disturb your content by accident.
  • It runs in your browser, signed in as you, and only ever talks to learn.uq.edu.au.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. The spreadsheet and the dashboard are built on your own machine and saved to your Downloads folder.
  • There is no AI involved in running it, and no outside code is loaded.
  • You only ever see the courses you already have access to.
Easiest way

AUse the button

No console, no pasting. You set this up once and then it is a button you press.

Drag the yellow bar below onto your bookmarks bar. If you cannot see your bookmarks bar, press Ctrl Shift B to show it.

Blackboard inventory

Drag it up to the bar. Clicking it here does nothing.

Then, whenever you want a report, open Blackboard, sign in, and click Blackboard inventory in your bookmarks bar. A small panel opens in the top right corner of the page.

Pick which courses you want, press Run, and watch the progress bar. When it finishes, the panel gives you two download buttons.

If no panel appears within a second or two, your browser has blocked the bookmarklet. Use Option B instead.

If the button is not an option

BPaste it into the console

The console is a panel built into your browser where you can run a piece of code. It looks alarming and is completely ordinary.

  1. Sign in to Blackboard in Chrome or Edge. Any Blackboard page will do.
  2. Press F12. A panel opens, usually on the right or along the bottom.
  3. Click the Console tab along the top of that panel.
  4. The first time only, Chrome will refuse to let you paste, as a safety measure. It will ask you to type allow pasting and press enter. Do that once and it will not ask again.
  5. Press the copy button below, click into the console, paste, and press enter.
  6. A panel opens in the top right of the page. Choose your courses, press Run, then use the download buttons when it finishes.

Choosing what to read

It does not sweep everything unless you ask it to.

The panel opens on the teaching period you are currently in, because that is almost always what you want. The dropdown lists your other terms with the number of courses in each, and the last option is every course you can see.

The box underneath narrows it further. Type BISM to get one discipline, or part of a course code for a single course. The green line tells you how many courses match and roughly how long it will take before you commit to anything.

Reading a couple of hundred courses takes a few minutes. Reading one semester usually takes well under a minute.

What you get

Two files, saved when you press the buttons in the panel.

A spreadsheet

Opens in Excel. One row per item, so you can sort and filter it however you like, or send it to someone who wants the raw list.

A dashboard

Opens in your browser. The same information with search, filters and totals, plus a list of assets that turn up in more than one course.

If something goes wrong

It says I am not allowed to paste

Chrome blocks pasting into the console until you tell it once that you understand. Type allow pasting into the console, press enter, then paste again.

I clicked the bookmark and nothing happened

The panel takes a moment to appear because it reads your course list first. If nothing shows up after a few seconds, your browser or a policy has blocked the bookmarklet, and Option B will still work.

Nothing downloaded

The files only save when you press the download buttons in the panel. If you pressed them and nothing arrived, check for a blocked download icon in the address bar.

It is taking ages

A sweep of a couple of hundred courses takes several minutes, which is normal. The progress bar shows how far through it is. Leave the tab open and let it work. If you just want a look at the output, pick a single term or type a course code into the filter box.

Some courses were skipped

Any course it could not read is listed at the bottom of the dashboard with the reason. Usually it is a course with unusual permissions, and it is safe to ignore.

My list is different to my colleague's

That is expected. It only sees the courses you have access to, so two people will get different results. Neither is the full institutional picture.
