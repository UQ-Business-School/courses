How should students form groups?

A group formation decision tool for assessment design

There is no single best approach to group formation. The most appropriate method depends on what students need to learn, what the assessment requires, and the context in which the group will work.

How to use this tool:
For each statement, select Important to this assessment or Not important to this assessment. Your responses will indicate which group formation approach may best fit your assessment.
1 of 8 · Diverse perspectives
Diverse perspectives are important to achieving the learning outcomes.
2 of 8 · Teamwork
Teamwork is an important skill being assessed.
3 of 8 · Working with unfamiliar people
Students need to develop the ability to work with unfamiliar people.
4 of 8 · Equity and inclusion
Equity and inclusion are important considerations when forming groups.
5 of 8 · Interdependence
Students need to depend on one another to successfully complete the assessment.
6 of 8 · Collaboration
Collaboration is an important part of demonstrating achievement in the assessment.
7 of 8 · Demonstrated skills
Students need to demonstrate their ability to collaborate with others.
8 of 8 · Working with peers
Students would benefit from working with peers they might not otherwise choose.

Your result

Before you decide

The result is a guide rather than a prescription. Consider the learning outcomes, assessment requirements, student experience, group composition and staff capacity before choosing your approach.

The tool uses your responses to identify the level of need for deliberate group formation. It does not determine whether groupwork itself is appropriate.
